Course Schedule

The course has twelve sessions and a capstone project. The table below lists, for each session, its page, its main reading, its biological case study and the size of its lab. Dates, times and rooms are in the Canvas schedule and calendar, which can also be subscribed to as https://course.bioinfo.se/kb8029.ics. How to get to SciLifeLab is on the Session 0 page. See Course Information & Logistics for grading, lab policy, and how the course actually runs.

Three phases: bioinformatics foundations build up to profiles and protein structure (Sessions 3-6); a convergence zone (Sessions 7-9) where the classical material directly motivates and is resolved by neural networks; then a deep-learning arc (Sessions 10-12) that keeps extending the same secondary-structure-prediction thread.

Every lab, regardless of the length noted below, is administered through Canvas, not this book — the “Lab” column here just indicates how substantial each session’s lab is, not where to find or submit it. See Course Information & Logistics for the general lab-quiz policy (50% correct to pass, one redo chance, each lab due the evening before the next lecture) and the course’s Canvas page for each lab’s actual worksheet, deadline, and submission mechanism.

3 Biological Databases Wikipedia: Biological database, Sequence database; NCBI: GenBank overview; practice via EMBL-EBI Training’s What is ENA? and The UniProt databases The HBB (beta-globin) locus Light exercise
4 Sequence Alignment Wikipedia: Needleman–Wunsch algorithm, Smith–Waterman algorithm, Substitution matrix (BLOSUM/PAM); practice: EMBL-EBI Pairwise Sequence Alignment and Multiple Sequence Alignment Aligning two homologous globins Full (by-hand DP matrix + traceback, then parameter/E-value sensitivity, then a simple MSA)
5 Advanced Database Search & Profile Methods (BLAST, PSSMs, HMMs) Wikipedia: BLAST (biotechnology), Hidden Markov model, Position weight matrix, Sequence profiling tool; NCBI: BLAST® Help (public domain) A real BLAST search on an unknown sequence, followed by PSI-BLAST/a Pfam domain search on the same family — framed explicitly as: a profile/PSSM score is a weighted sum over positions, i.e. already a linear model Full (BLAST and profile/HMM search)
6 Protein Structure Wikipedia: Protein structure; RCSB PDB-101 Hemoglobin’s 3D fold in PyMOL: secondary structure (helices, strands) and tertiary structure, the structural background for Session 7’s secondary-structure prediction Full (PyMOL)
7 Linear models, gradient descent, the XOR problem & secondary structure prediction d2l.ai, Linear Neural Networks for Regression; Wikipedia Perceptron, Perceptrons (book), Protein secondary structure; Bulyk, Johnson & Church (2002), Nucleic Acids Research 30(5):1255-1261; Rost & Sander (1993), J. Mol. Biol. 232:584-599 Formalize Session 5’s profile score as a perceptron, and predict the secondary structure that Session 6 introduced; show its weights can be learned by gradient descent instead of derived from alignment statistics; two real-world punchlines — a zinc-finger transcription factor’s non-additive binding (Bulyk et al.), and Rost & Sander’s PHD method solving real secondary-structure prediction the same way, at scale Full
8 Machine learning foundations & practical PyTorch d2l.ai, Linear Neural Networks for Classification + Builders’ Guide Regression vs. classification, evaluation metrics, homology-aware splits (MMseqs2); subcellular localization network defined in PyTorch Full (evaluating a blood-cell classifier)
9 How a neural network learns, and why training fails d2l.ai, Multilayer Perceptrons Training a network on breast-cancer biopsies; why training fails (momentum and Adam); epistasis in the GB1 protein fitness landscape (when a hidden layer pays off); overfitting on protein localization; L1 and L2 regularization Full (training loop on blood-cell images)
10 CNNs, from ImageNet to biology d2l.ai, Convolutional Neural Networks + Modern Convolutional Neural Networks ImageNet history, cancer histology with transfer learning and an image foundation model (DINOv2), biomedical imaging, DNA (DeepSEA), a learned signal-peptide filter, contact prediction Full (CNNs on blood-cell images)
11 Sequences, language models & SignalP d2l.ai, Recurrent Neural Networks + Attention Mechanisms and Transformers RNN/LSTM, attention and the Transformer, the road to GPT and BERT, protein language models as foundation models; SignalP 1.0 → 6.0 and its few-shot gain, reproduced with ESM-2 Full (RNNs and ESM embeddings)
12 AlphaFold & generative models of protein structure Primary papers (AlphaFold 1-3, ColabFold, RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN) From coevolution to AlphaFold; complexes (pDockQ, ipTM, ipSAE); AlphaFold3 and diffusion; the four regions of interaction prediction; protein design Full (AlphaFold Server, AlphaFold DB)
