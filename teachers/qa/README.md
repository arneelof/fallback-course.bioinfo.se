## The idea

A Q&A is not a repeat of the lectures. Listening to the same explanation again feels useful but
does little for the exam; trying to recall the material, and finding out what you cannot yet
do, does much more (retrieval practice). So each Q&A is a practice exam with immediate
feedback: students answer exam-style questions on paper, mark each other's answers against
the marking guide, and the teacher spends the teaching time on what the room actually got
wrong.

It adds no pressure: nothing is handed in or graded, the poll is anonymous, and
students keep their own answers. The reason to come is that it is the best exam preparation in
the course: the exam's format, with the marking guide, so that students see what a full answer
looks like. Say so when announcing it.

## The two hours

| Time | Minutes | What | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | 3 | **Start.** The logged-in check-in (`live-quiz/content/qaN-checkin.md`, a normal session, one question): answering it counts as attending, and attending makes up for one missed lecture. Why we do it this way (one slide: "recall beats rereading"); nothing else is recorded. Then start the anonymous poll (*Run anonymously*) and open its last round ("A question you want answered now") so students can add questions all session. | Teacher |
| 0:03 | 7 | **Brain dump.** Books and notes closed. Each student writes everything they remember about the two or three topics given in the marking guide. No discussion. | Students |
| 0:10 | 37 | **Practice exam.** Hand out the practice exam (4 questions, 5 points each). Individually, on paper, with the same aids as the exam (their five pages of notes). Give a 10-minute and a 2-minute warning. | Students; teacher and TAs only clarify the wording |
| 0:47 | 15 | **Break.** Hand out the marking guide as students leave, or put it on the screen when they come back. Teacher: glance at the questions posted so far. | |
| 1:02 | 20 | **Marking in pairs.** Students swap papers and mark each other's answers against the marking guide, then discuss where they disagree. TAs go round: settle disputed marks, explain the model answers, and note which questions most pairs struggle with. | Students; TAs circulate |
| 1:22 | 30 | **What the room got wrong.** Run the anonymous poll (hardest question, questions with less than half the points, sessions least ready for). Ask the TAs what they saw. Go through the two or three weakest points at the blackboard, then answer the most common posted questions. | Teacher |
| 1:52 | 8 | **What to study now.** Each student writes down their two weakest topics and where to read about them (the session pages), and keeps the note. Close: where past exams are, and that the TAs and the forum take questions until the exam. | Students, teacher |

120 minutes in all. If something runs over, shorten the teacher's 30 minutes, never the practice
exam or the marking: those are the parts that do the work.

## Attendance and the bonus points

Attending a Q&A makes up for one missed lecture. The check-in session is a normal, logged-in
quiz.bioinfo.se session, so it is in the attendance export to Canvas (Reports, the Canvas
participation CSV) like a lecture; the anonymous poll is left out of it. The export then counts
up to 14 sessions (12 lectures and 2 Q&As), and the lecture bonus is
3 - max(0, 12 - sessions attended), and at least 0.

## Brief for the TAs

- During the practice exam: clarify the wording of a question if asked, but do not hint at the answer.
- During the marking: walk round the pairs. Settle disagreements about marks using the marking
  guide; where the guide is unclear, give the benefit of the doubt and tell the teacher. Explain
  a model answer to a pair that does not understand it.
- Note which question most pairs struggle with and the most common mistake, and tell the
  teacher before 1:22: that, with the poll, decides what the teacher goes through.
- Do not collect or record anything.

## What students are told

On the Course Information page (Bonus points and exam section) and in the Canvas calendar
event: the Q&A is a practice exam with a marking guide, not a lecture; bring your notes (the same
pages you will bring to the exam), pen and paper; nothing is graded or collected; send questions
on the course forum before, or post them anonymously during the session.
