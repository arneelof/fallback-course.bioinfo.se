Session 3: lecturer’s timetable

Biological Databases · KB8029 · Suggested three-hour lecture plan

The lecture has three 50-minute blocks and two 15-minute breaks, corresponding to the three learning goals in the student reading. Use guided demonstrations with pauses for questions. The final block protects about 10 minutes for questions submitted during preparation and 5–10 minutes for a short live quiz.

The live quiz provides the conceptual checks previously planned as classroom activities. Allow explanations and questions to take the time the class needs. The optional lab practice provides time for independent record mapping, evidence investigation, sequence retrieval and documentation.

Teaching notes

Block 1: databases, records and evidence

The existing lecture’s slides 3–32 supply the main material. Organise progressive browser screenshots around the decisions students make. Keep the resource catalogue brief enough to explain the HBB records slowly and pause for questions.

Use slides 5–7 as one sequence: slide 5 introduces INSDC cooperation; slide 6 shows different interfaces to shared records, linked record types and submission through one appropriate partner; slide 7 shows why archives and access routes can change. Explain the roles of project/study, sample, sequencing-read and assembly records. An assembly is a reconstructed sequence deposited by researchers, rather than something the archive automatically makes from a sample submission. These roles describe an investigation and its data processing, while DNA, RNA and protein describe biological levels.

Use the Trace Archive example to teach recovery from an obsolete link: retain the accession, consult current provider guidance and check the retrieved record’s identity and metadata. Students do not need to memorise retirement dates or lists of former archive names. Daily exchange does not guarantee immediate website availability, and the same submitted record at several partner sites is not independent evidence.

Show how human HBB, NCBI Gene 3043, RefSeq transcript NM_000518.5, protein NP_000509.1 and UniProt P68871 are connected. Explain what each identifier represents and what a sequence version specifies. For one biological claim, display its evidence or linked reference. Explain that curation can evaluate both experimental and predicted information.

Use slides 8–12 to connect biological levels. The DNA pictures are different scales of the same genomic material. RNA records describe transcript sequences, non-coding RNAs and expression measurements. Protein resources connect sequences with structure, domains, interactions, abundance and location. The named databases illustrate these categories without forming an exhaustive list.

For the revised data-level slides, select live-quiz questions 1, 3, 6, 7 and 8: shared archival records, claim-level evidence, linked transcript records, RNA sequence notation and expression measurements. Questions 2, 4 and 5 remain alternatives for identifiers, integrated access and changing archive routes. These checks require no database searching or API calls during the quiz. Independent record mapping, retrieval choices and evidence investigation belong in lab practice, where students can work at their own pace.

Block 2: three access routes and sequence retrieval

Use the existing slides 33–65 as a basis for the demonstration, comparing the routes for the same target:

  1. Command-line or programmatic access: a tool or program queries a particular database, often through an API, and can automate repeated retrieval.
  2. Database-specific web pages: interactive searching and inspection of that database’s records.
  3. Genome browsers: one web interface integrating genomic, transcript and protein information, so these levels can be accessed without separately searching each level’s database website.

Genome browsers are themselves web interfaces. Their value here is that they bring several biological levels together while retaining the gene and selected transcript as the common context.

Use human HBB on GRCh38/hg38 in UCSC, then compare the same target briefly in Ensembl. Search UCSC for HBB. The gene identifiers are NCBI Gene 3043 and Ensembl ENSG00000244734.

For the selected coding transcript, use RefSeq NM_000518.5 / NP_000509.1 or the corresponding Ensembl pair ENST00000335295.4 / ENSP00000333994.3. Show the annotation source and record versions actually retrieved. HBB is on the minus strand, so explain the orientation chosen for genomic output.

Demonstrate a genomic sequence spanning the selected transcript, the full spliced transcript/cDNA and its encoded protein. Explain the output headers and document organism, assembly, annotation source, genomic interval and orientation, transcript/protein versions and sequence types in a retrieval log. Show a prepared API request and response when comparing access routes.

Question 4 is available as an alternative live-quiz check of integrated access. Students carry out the downloads and produce their own retrieval log during lab practice.

Block 3: literature, submitted questions and live quiz

Use the first 30 minutes for guided literature discovery, drawing on selected examples from the existing slides 66–79. PubMed is the worked example for constructing and checking a query.

  1. 5 minutes: frame a biological question, compare search routes and distinguish a citation from access to full text.
  2. 12 minutes: construct and refine a PubMed query, inspect Search Details and follow a full-text link.
  3. 8 minutes: show a prepared AI-assisted search suggesting terms or papers, and compare it with the database search.
  4. 5 minutes: check one suggested reference and one supporting passage or result together, with time for questions.

Use the role of HBB variants in sickle-cell disease as the shared PubMed and AI question. Distinguish establishing a paper’s identity from checking whether it supports a claimed finding. Show the prepared output and source so students can follow the example without an AI account.

Group duplicate preparation questions beforehand and reserve about 10 minutes for the recurring themes.

Short final live quiz

Download the eight-question source for import into the quiz teacher dashboard. Choose five questions before class and leave the others unopened. For the revised data-level slides, use 1, 3, 6, 7 and 8, with the shared HBB archival-record question first. Each question uses mode: once: one vote followed by the result and a brief explanation.

The source title is Session 3: Biological Databases (short lecture quiz). If a quiz with that title has already been used, import a newly titled revision so its recorded responses are retained. These local edits do not update the running quiz service.

Keep university sign-in for the course’s attendance policy. Project aggregate results, invite voluntary questions and avoid selecting a named student to explain an answer. Correctness is never graded. Allow thinking time and avoid competitive scoring. An anonymous session would not create attendance records.

Eight available conceptual checks: select five, using the recommended set for slides 8–12
QuestionSelectionGoalAnswer and explanation
1. What does the same HBB accession and sequence at NCBI and ENA represent?Recommended1Two access routes to one shared archival record. INSDC partners exchange public records under shared accessions; agreement between their interfaces does not provide independent experimental support for the annotation.
2. Which identifier includes the HBB mRNA sequence version?Alternative1NM_000518.5. The gene symbol and Gene ID identify the gene; NP_000509.1 is a protein record.
3. How should a claim in a reviewed UniProt entry be assessed?Recommended1Inspect that claim’s evidence and references. Reviewed status describes curation; it does not establish experimental support for every claim.
4. What does a genome browser offer for the HBB retrieval task?Alternative2Connected genomic, transcript and encoded-protein views within one resource. Organism, assembly and transcript choice still matter.
5. What should you do when an old Trace Archive link fails?Alternative1; apply 2Keep the accession, consult current provider guidance and verify the record’s metadata. A retired interface does not by itself establish that the underlying data have been deleted; another website showing the same accession is not a separate experiment.
6. How can two transcript records be connected to the same gene?Recommended1Check their linked gene identifier within the same database, organism and annotation release. Different transcript identifiers can link to one gene. A shared identifier prefix or equal sequence length does not establish that connection.
7. Why can a non-coding RNA record use T instead of U?Recommended1The DNA alphabet can represent an RNA sequence, including a non-coding RNA. This notation alone does not establish that a physical cDNA sample was made or that the sequence is genomic DNA.
8. What is needed to estimate HBB RNA abundance in a blood sample?Recommended1Expression measurements from the relevant sample or condition. A sequence record identifies a sequence; it does not establish how much of that RNA occurs in a particular sample. Reopening the same sequence through another archive adds no abundance measurement.

Optional oral prompt for goal 3, if time remains: “An AI tool cites a real paper with a valid DOI. What must you check before using its claimed finding?” Expected response: open the paper and check the passage or result supporting the claim. A valid reference alone does not establish that support.

Keep the quiz within 5–10 minutes by asking the selected five questions, including joining and brief explanations. The three alternatives are substitutions, not extra rounds. If discussion needs longer, take remaining questions into the lab handover.

Lab practice

The student lab-practice guidance contains the full HBB record/evidence and retrieval tasks. These are optional, ungraded practice at the students’ own pace. The programmatic notebook demonstrates bounded examples from supplied identifiers and prepared outputs. Students run and interpret these examples before choosing and checking records independently in the lab and assembling their own dossier. The notebook’s one-record, two-interface exercise compares the submitted HBB mRNA record V00497.1 through NCBI and ENA; it does not treat the RefSeq transcript as a mirrored INSDC submission. The graded Canvas lab retains its own requirements: a reproducible human HBB record log followed by the myoglobin (MB) transfer dossier, including record types, one followed cross-reference, retrieval dates and evidence.

Before the lecture

  • Review preparation questions and group recurring themes.
  • Prepare the HBB record walkthrough, sequence outputs and retrieval log, with saved examples available for the demonstration.
  • Check HBB links and prepare the short command-line/API example.
  • Check the current Trace Archive access guidance and prepare a saved record example, so the demonstration does not depend on following an obsolete link in class.
  • Prepare an AI-assisted literature example with its tool, prompt and date, a checked reference and the relevant supporting passage.
  • Preview the eight-question source and select five questions (recommended: 1, 3, 6, 7 and 8). Keep question 1 first, check sign-in and joining, and allow 5–10 minutes for the session.
