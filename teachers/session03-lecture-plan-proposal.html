Session 3: Biological Databases

Lecture timetable · KB8029

The lecture has three 50-minute teaching blocks and two 15-minute breaks. Each block follows one of the three learning goals. The final block reserves 10 minutes for preparation questions and 10 minutes for the live quiz. Independent searches and dossier building belong in the lab; the lecture uses paced demonstrations with pauses for questions.

Follow one clear route through HBB and show selected alternatives briefly. Keep the archive history (slide 7), the UniProt overview (slide 24) and extra AI failure examples in reserve if time is short.

Before the lecture

  • Ask students to read the three goals, the database and record concepts, and the browser/literature introductions. The notebook is a bounded demonstration, not an additional dossier assignment.
  • Group the submitted preparation questions by theme. Use a brief clarification at the start if it unblocks the lecture, while preserving the 10-minute discussion near the end.
  • Prepare saved browser views, sequence exports, query results and the relevant paper figure. Live services and AI output can change, so the story must also work from saved examples.
  • Load the ten live quiz questions in Quizbinf before class.

What to protect when discussion takes longer

Keep the distinction between shared records and independent evidence, the need for versioned identifiers, one complete sequence-export example, one variant-to-evidence route, and the two checks on AI-suggested literature. Shorten extra resource lists, repeated browser zooms and extra query variants first. Protect the final submitted-question period and the quiz; use remaining examples in the lab or reading.

Quiz and lab handover

The ten-question quiz is ready for Quizbinf import with one round per question. Use the full 10-minute slot and keep the existing sign-in and participation policy. The quiz is ungraded.

Students apply the lecture workflow to human myoglobin. They use the notebook to check sequence records, then use database websites to examine the protein and trace a reported interaction to its experimental evidence. The graded lab instructions sets out the three-hour schedule and Canvas questions.
