About this lab

The lab follows the five questions of the Session 3 page: where is the sequence, what is known about the protein, which family does it belong to, is there a structure, and what does it interact with? For every record you open, ask two things: which identifier am I looking at, and who vouches for this information (the submitters, a curator, a prediction program, or an experiment)? The lab ends with a database dossier of your own.

You may discuss methods and approaches with your peers, but the answer you submit must be your own. See Course Information & Logistics for this course’s general lab-quiz policy (50% correct to pass, one redo chance if you fail, same deadline for every lab).

Identifiers and versions

The INSDC partners operate GenBank (NCBI, USA), the European Nucleotide Archive (ENA, EMBL-EBI) and DDBJ (Japan). Researchers submit through one appropriate partner; the partners exchange public records daily and share accessions, although release and indexing can delay their appearance at a particular website. Different interfaces to the same submitted record are not independent evidence. Accessions identify records; a sequence accession with its version (the number after the dot) specifies a sequence version; genomic coordinates also need an assembly, annotation source and coordinate convention.

Optional preparation (ungraded): try One submitted record, two partner interfaces in the Session 3 notebook. It compares the submitted human HBB mRNA record V00497.1 at NCBI and ENA. Check the accession and version, organism, sequence length and sequence before deciding that two pages describe the same record. This archival record is distinct from the RefSeq transcript used below. Project, sample, read-run and assembly records describe related parts of an investigation; they are not interchangeable sequence records. If an old archive link fails, keep its identifier and consult the provider’s current access guidance before concluding that the data are unavailable.

Submitted, predicted and curated records

RefSeq is NCBI’s non-redundant collection built from INSDC data. Its non-predicted records have N-prefixes (NM_, NP_; many of them curated); records predicted by NCBI’s genome-annotation pipeline have X-prefixes (XM_, XP_). UniProtKB has two sections: Swiss-Prot (reviewed by curators) and TrEMBL (unreviewed, annotated automatically). This part uses CDC28, the gene for baker’s yeast’s main cyclin-dependent kinase (the counterpart of human CDK1).

Which family does it belong to?

InterPro integrates Pfam and more than a dozen other family and domain databases. A protein’s InterPro page shows which families and domains it contains.

Is there a structure?

The PDB holds experimentally determined structures; its entries record the method and, for X-ray and cryo-EM, the resolution. The AlphaFold Protein Structure Database holds predicted models, with a per-residue confidence score (pLDDT, 0-100) that can vary a lot along one protein. The archaeal protein complex 2WNR (Methanothermobacter thermautotrophicus) is used to move between a structure and its genes.

Question 9 · choose one · 1 point

2WNR was solved at 2.65 Å resolution. A residue in an AlphaFold model has pLDDT 92. What does each number tell you?

  • The resolution describes how much detail the experimental data on the real molecule could resolve; the pLDDT is the prediction’s own estimate of how reliable the model is at that residue

  • Both measure the same thing; 92 is simply a better score than 2.65

  • The pLDDT says how well the model fits the X-ray data of 2WNR

  • The resolution is a prediction confidence; the pLDDT is an experimental measurement

What does it interact with?

IntAct curates individual physical interactions from published experiments; Complex Portal curates stable complexes with their composition. STRING combines curated interactions, co-expression, conserved gene neighbourhood, predictions and text mining into one association score. CDC28 partners with different cyclins in different phases of the cell cycle.

Question 10 · short answer · 1 point

Question 11 · choose one · 1 point

Search STRING for CDC28 in Saccharomyces cerevisiae and open one of its high-scoring partners. Compared with an IntAct entry, what does a STRING score represent?

  • A combined confidence that two proteins are functionally associated, from several kinds of evidence (experiments, co-expression, databases, text mining, predictions), not necessarily a measured physical contact

  • The number of crystal structures in which the two proteins are seen together

  • A physical interaction that has always been confirmed by at least two experiments

  • The sequence identity between the two proteins

Literature

PubMed indexes citations and abstracts. Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT), exact phrases in quotation marks, and field tags in square brackets (e.g. [title], [author], [publication type]) sharpen a search; see NCBI’s search field documentation. Before each search, predict roughly what you expect; afterwards, check whether the result makes sense.
