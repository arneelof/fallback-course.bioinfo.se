Beyond Step 1 -- affine gaps

Step 1 used a flat, linear gap penalty – the same cost per gap position, however many are strung together. The book page’s own “Gap penalties: linear vs. affine” section explains why a real aligner instead uses an affine penalty: a larger one-time cost to open a new gap (), plus a smaller cost per additional position in that same run (), for a run of consecutive gaps – charged once per contiguous run, not once per gap position. This makes one long gap cheaper than the same number of gaps scattered as separate short ones, which is usually more realistic, since evolutionary insertions and deletions often affect contiguous stretches.

Score this alignment by hand with match = +2, mismatch = -1, = -4, = -1 (apply the formula above once per contiguous gap run):

seqX: V M M M V A H - - F G
seqY: V M - - V I H W H Y G
